The other half of radar: not how much echo comes back (amplitude) but how far it travelled (phase). After this page you know what InSAR delivers for flood mapping – elevation models and displacement – and which DEMs to use in GEE.

SAR interferometry

SAR interferometry (InSAR) is a technique developed to infer 3-D information from pairs of SAR images. Unlike the backscatter analysis of the previous page, it uses the phase information of the SAR data. InSAR is extremely sensitive to ground features: it measures topographic height with an accuracy of a few metres and ground displacement with millimetre accuracy (subsidence, landslides – lecture 5, earthquakes, dams).

Elevation models from InSAR

One contribution of InSAR to flood monitoring is accurate topographic information over large areas. Digital elevation data for most of the land surface were first obtained by the NASA Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM), flown in 2000 aboard the Space Shuttle Endeavour. A second-generation global digital elevation model (DEM) was acquired by the TanDEM-X mission of the German space agency (DLR). The constellation shown below consists of the two satellites TanDEM-X and TerraSAR-X; the derived global DEM has a 12.5 m posting and is the basis of the free Copernicus DEM (30 m).

What a DEM gives a flood map

  • a slope mask – steep slopes cannot hold flood water, so dark pixels there are radar shadow, not water (used in the lab script);
  • flow accumulation and HAND (height above nearest drainage) for exposure analysis;
  • flood depth = water surface minus terrain height.
