Milestone M6 – the burn severity map and report from lab 6 (21.12), due 11.01, at the start of lab 7. One .pdf or .docx report written to the template below, with the link to your saved script.

What to submit

  1. Your fire: start and end dates from FIRMS hotspots; cloud-free pre- and post-fire Sentinel-2 dates from Copernicus Browser; the perimeter from EFFIS or Copernicus EMS.
  2. Run the burn severity script on COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED with your pre and post windows and your AOI; check in the Console how many images were found in each window.
  3. Classify dNBR with the USGS thresholds (−250, −100, 100, 270, 440, 660 on the × 1000 scale), add a legend and compute the area per class in hectares.
  4. Compare the perimeter with EFFIS or EMS: where do they disagree and why (clouds, phenology, agricultural fields)?
  5. Export the severity map as GeoTIFF (scale 20 m) and write the report with the checklist of lesson page 3.
  6. Optional: an NDVI or NBR time series over the perimeter for the years after the fire (recovery).

The step-by-step instructions are in the lesson fire detection and severity estimation.

Report template – the chapters of your report

Use these headings in this order (3–6 pages; maps as numbered figures with captions). The same template is used for every homework of the course, so the points you learn here count every time.

  1. Title and metadata – event or area, dates, your name, the link to the saved script (test it in a private browser window).
  2. Aim – one sentence: what was mapped and why.
  3. Data – collections and their IDs, acquisition dates, the AOI (area in km², how it was defined), reference data.
    • FIRMS timeline (first and last hotspot), pre and post windows and the number of Sentinel-2 images in each
    • the band used for NIR (B8 or B8A) and SWIR2 (B12); EFFIS/EMS perimeter with its date
  4. Method – the steps in the order of the script; every parameter and threshold with a one-sentence justification.
    • the cloud mask, NBR and dNBR formulas, the × 1000 scaling
    • the USGS class thresholds and a remark on their origin (US forests)
  5. Results – the map(s) with legend, scale bar and source line; the numbers (tables in hectares, accuracies, counts).
    • the burn severity map with the USGS legend and the perimeter overlaid
    • the area per class in hectares, summing up to the AOI area
    • the dNBR histogram over the perimeter
  6. Validation and uncertainty – comparison with a reference and the sensitivity test where required.
    • the comparison with the EFFIS/EMS perimeter (area inside/outside, reasons for disagreement)
    • the unburned class outside the perimeter as a check of the phenology match
  7. Limitations and conclusions – clouds, phenology, resolution, speckle, sample quality – and two or three sentences on what the map can and cannot be used for.

Before you upload

The unburned class covers the area outside the perimeter; the dNBR histogram over the perimeter has its unburned peak near 0; the sum of the class areas equals the AOI area; the script link opens in a private browser window.
