The radar half of the lesson. After this page you understand why calm water is dark in a Sentinel-1 image, what VV and VH mean, and you can load, filter and display Sentinel-1 before/after images in GEE – the first part of the UN-SPIDER script.

Why SAR for floods

Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) data are fundamental data sources for flood mapping. Due to their longer wavelengths (C-band of Sentinel-1: about 5.6 cm) SAR images are not affected by cloud cover, severe weather or lighting conditions – they work through the clouds that caused the flood, day and night. Learn more: Introduction to SAR (ASF).

  • The satellite emits microwave pulses sideways and records the echo – the backscatter (σ⁰, in dB).
  • Polarisation: Sentinel-1 over land transmits vertically and receives vertically (VV) or horizontally (VH). VH is sensitive to volume scattering (vegetation), VV to surface roughness.
  • Side-looking geometry causes shadow and layover in mountains; speckle (salt-and-pepper noise) must always be filtered.

What the echo tells us

Calm water strongly reflects the radar pulse away from the receiving antenna (specular reflection), so a backscatter analysis is a simple yet effective way of flood monitoring: water-covered areas appear darker than land, and image thresholding identifies water by its very low backscatter intensity.

SurfaceMechanismBackscatter
calm waterspecular – pulse bounces away from the antennavery low (dark, below about −18 dB)
bare soil, grasssurface scattering, roughnessmedium
forest, cropsvolume scatteringmedium–high (especially VH)
buildingsdouble bounce (wall + ground)very high (bright)
flooded forest / citydouble bounce water + trunk or wallbrighter than before
wind-roughened watersurface scatteringbrighter – false "land"

Reasons for non-compliance with the "water is dark" rule therefore include wind, which roughens the water surface, the presence of vegetation, and surrounding walls or other man-made structures that cause double-bounce reflections and increase the backscatter.

Loading Sentinel-1 in GEE

Sentinel-1 (today 1C and 1D – 1C launched Dec 2024, 1D launched Nov 2025 and operational since May 2026; 1A worked from 2014 to June 2026 – 6-day repeat with two satellites) is in the catalogue as COPERNICUS/S1_GRD, bands VV, VH, angle, values already in dB. Main mode over land: IW (Interferometric Wide), 10 m GRD pixels (amplitude only, terrain-corrected).

  1. Filter the collection – one mode, one polarisation, one orbit direction (never mix ascending and descending in change detection): 
    var s1 = ee.ImageCollection('COPERNICUS/S1_GRD')
  .filter(ee.Filter.eq('instrumentMode', 'IW'))
  .filter(ee.Filter.listContains('transmitterReceiverPolarisation', 'VH'))
  .filter(ee.Filter.eq('orbitProperties_pass', 'DESCENDING'))
  .filterBounds(aoi)
  .select('VH');
  2. Make a before and an after mosaic around the flood peak: 
    var before = s1.filterDate('2024-08-20', '2024-09-10').mosaic();
var after  = s1.filterDate('2024-09-15', '2024-09-25').mosaic();
  3. Reduce speckle with a 50 m focal mean and display both images: 
    var smooth = 50;
var beforeF = before.focal_mean(smooth, 'circle', 'meters');
var afterF  = after.focal_mean(smooth, 'circle', 'meters');
Map.addLayer(beforeF, {min: -25, max: 0}, 'before VH');
Map.addLayer(afterF,  {min: -25, max: 0}, 'after VH');
  4. Read values with the Inspector over known water and known land: the histogram is bimodal, with a water peak around −22 dB and a land peak around −12 dB. Choose VH for floods in open land (bigger land/water contrast) and VV for flooded vegetation (double bounce).

You are done when…

…the "after" layer shows new dark areas along the river that are absent in the "before" layer, and you have noted a typical dB value for water and for land.
