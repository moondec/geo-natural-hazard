Two applications of machine learning in GEE to natural hazards – landslides and wildfire severity. They are a preview of labs 5 and 6; today just open the scripts and see how the method you learned (thresholds and classifiers on a band stack) is used in published research.

1. Landslides – density heatmaps from Sentinel-1 (lab 5)

The GEE script links on this page are Earth Engine share links and open only when you are logged in to the Code Editor. Public alternatives (the authors' GitHub repository, the UN-SPIDER recommended practice) are listed next to them.

  • GEE script: Landslides
  • Scientific article: Handwerger et al. (2022), Generating landslide density heatmaps for rapid detection using open-access satellite radar data in Google Earth Engine, NHESS 22, 753–773 – SAR change and thresholds, no training data.
  • Public code: GitHub repository of the authors (scripts for every figure of the paper, no share link needed)

2. Wildfire – burn severity (lab 6)

  • GEE script: Wildfire
  • Scientific article: Al-hasn, R. & Almuhammad, R. (2022). Burned area determination using Sentinel-2 satellite images and the impact of fire on the availability of soil nutrients in Syria. J. For. Sci. 68(3), 96–106, doi:10.17221/122/2021-JFS – dNBR / BAIS2 thresholds, no machine learning; the Random Forest classification of damage classes is the lab's own extension.
  • Webinar: FU Berlin GEO-IT webinar
  • Public alternative: UN-SPIDER recommended practice – burn severity in GEE (importable script with line-by-line comments; used in lab 6)

A ready reference layer

Dynamic World (Google / WRI) does the same task as this week's exercise – per-pixel land cover from Sentinel-2 – but with a convolutional neural network instead of a Random Forest (Brown et al. 2022, Scientific Data 9, 251). It is in the catalogue as GOOGLE/DYNAMICWORLD/V1 and is a convenient reference layer to compare your classification against in the report.
