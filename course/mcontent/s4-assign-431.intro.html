Milestone M4 – the flood extent map from lab 4, due at the start of lab 5 (date and time above). Submit one .pdf or .docx report written to the template below, with the link to your copy of the UN-SPIDER flood script.

What to submit

  1. Pick a documented flood event (Copernicus Browser highlights, EMS activation, or south-west Poland, September 2024) and define the AOI.
  2. Run the Sentinel-1 change-detection script (lesson page ICEYE / the lab scripts): before and after periods, polarisation, orbit pass, threshold 1.25, speckle filter, masks.
  3. Produce the flood extent map with the event name and dates, and the flooded area in hectares.
  4. Sensitivity test: repeat with the thresholds 1.15 and 1.40 and with another filter radius (e.g. 30 m and 100 m); put the flooded areas in a table.
  5. Compare your map with the Global Flood Monitoring product or an EMS map of the same event – one paragraph of agreement and disagreement.
  6. Optional: the optical MNDWI map (script 1) for the same event and the two areas side by side.

The step-by-step instructions are in the lesson flood mapping.

Report template – the chapters of your report

Use these headings in this order (3–6 pages; maps as numbered figures with captions). The same template is used for every homework of the course, so the points you learn here count every time.

  1. Title and metadata – event or area, dates, your name, the link to the saved script (test it in a private browser window).
  2. Aim – one sentence: what was mapped and why.
  3. Data – collections and their IDs, acquisition dates, the AOI (area in km², how it was defined), reference data.
    • COPERNICUS/S1_GRD: before period and after period (dates), polarisation (VH/VV), orbit pass (ascending/descending), number of scenes in each period
    • reference: GFM or EMS product used, with its date
  4. Method – the steps in the order of the script; every parameter and threshold with a one-sentence justification.
    • speckle filter radius, difference threshold (1.25) and what it means in dB
    • the masks: permanent water (JRC GSW), slope, connected pixels
  5. Results – the map(s) with legend, scale bar and source line; the numbers (tables in hectares, accuracies, counts).
    • the flood extent map (legend: flooded / permanent water), event name and dates in the title
    • the flooded area in hectares
  6. Validation and uncertainty – comparison with a reference and the sensitivity test where required.
    • the sensitivity table: threshold (1.15 / 1.25 / 1.40) × filter radius versus flooded area, and how much the area changes (factor)
    • the comparison with GFM or EMS: where do the maps agree and disagree, and why (wind, flooded vegetation, urban areas, shadow)
  7. Limitations and conclusions – clouds, phenology, resolution, speckle, sample quality – and two or three sentences on what the map can and cannot be used for.

Before you upload

The flood layer lies along the river and not on mountain slopes; permanent lakes are not counted; the area changes by less than a factor of two between the thresholds 1.15 and 1.40; the script link opens in a private browser window.
