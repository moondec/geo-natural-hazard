Homework after lab 2 (23.11): repeat the lesson workflow (filter → median → index → classes → area table) for an index of your choice and your own area of interest. Due 30.11, at the start of lab 3. You submit a link to a working Earth Engine script and a short report (.pdf or .docx, 2–3 pages) written to the template below.

What to submit

  1. Create your own JavaScript code for Earth Engine that calculates the area occupied by the categories of a selected index (e.g. EVI, NDWI, SAVI, NDMI – not NDVI).
  2. Specify an area of interest (AOI) that is not very large: a city, a district or a national park (about 50–500 km²).
  3. Choose your own thresholds and justify them in one or two sentences (histogram, Inspector values, literature).
  4. Save the script, click Get Link and paste the link in the report and in the submission comment.
  5. Upload the report as .pdf or .docx.

The step-by-step instructions are in the lesson Google Earth Engine.

Report template – the chapters of your report

Use these headings in this order (3–6 pages; maps as numbered figures with captions). The same template is used for every homework of the course, so the points you learn here count every time.

  1. Title and metadata – event or area, dates, your name, the link to the saved script (test it in a private browser window).
  2. Aim – one sentence: what was mapped and why.
  3. Data – collections and their IDs, acquisition dates, the AOI (area in km², how it was defined), reference data.
    • the collection (COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED), the date range and the cloud filter used
    • the AOI: how you defined it (polygon, point + buffer, GAUL district)
  4. Method – the steps in the order of the script; every parameter and threshold with a one-sentence justification.
    • the index formula and its bands
    • the cloud mask you applied (SCL, Cloud Score+)
    • the thresholds and the class codes
    • the scale used in reduceRegion
  5. Results – the map(s) with legend, scale bar and source line; the numbers (tables in hectares, accuracies, counts).
    • the index map and the class map with a legend
    • the area table per class in hectares
  6. Validation and uncertainty – comparison with a reference and the sensitivity test where required.
    • three pixels checked with the Inspector (field, forest, water) and whether they fall in the expected class
    • what happens to the areas when a threshold moves by ±0.05
  7. Limitations and conclusions – clouds, phenology, resolution, speckle, sample quality – and two or three sentences on what the map can and cannot be used for.
