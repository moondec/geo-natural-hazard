Lab 6 maps burn severity with Sentinel-2. This page fixes the vocabulary – intensity, severity, soil burn severity – and shows which of the three satellite fire products needs a before/after image pair. Pages 2–3 explain the spectral basis and the dNBR classes; page 4 is the exercise.

Vocabulary first

Fire intensity

  • The amount of energy or heat released per unit time or area; it encompasses several specific measures.
  • Byram (1959), fireline intensity: "The rate of energy or heat release per unit time, per unit length of fire front, regardless of its depth." Review of the terms: Keeley (2009), Int. J. Wildland Fire 18, 116–126.
  • Physics of the flames, measured during the fire (kW/m); from satellites: Fire Radiative Power (FRP, MW).

Fire (burn) severity

  • The effect of a fire on ecosystem properties, often defined by the degree of mortality of vegetation and consumption of organic matter.
  • The degree to which a site has been altered or disrupted by fire; loosely, a product of fire intensity and residence time. Measured after the fire – this is what dNBR maps.

Soil burn severity

  • The fire-induced changes in physical, chemical and biological soil properties that affect hydrological and biological soil functions; it drives erosion and post-fire floods or debris flows (the link to lab 5).

Burned area

  • The perimeter or the pixels that burned, regardless of severity.

Remember

Active fire tells where it burns now; burned area tells how much; severity tells how badly – and only the last one needs the before/after pair. The FIRMS hotspot dates tell you when the fire burned, so they define your post-fire window.
