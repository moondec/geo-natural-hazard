Lab 4 (7.12). This lesson explains how water is seen by optical satellites (water indices) and by radar (Sentinel-1 backscatter), and on its last page it guides you through the UN-SPIDER flood mapping script. At the end you will have a flood extent map of a real event, the flooded area and a table of how that area changes with the threshold (milestone M4).

Learning objectives

  • Know which multispectral bands and satellites are used to monitor floods.
  • Learn the water body mapping indices (NDWI, MNDWI and others) and their limits.
  • Understand the relation between water bodies and SAR backscatter.
  • Gain an understanding of InSAR and DEMs for flood mapping.
  • Run a Sentinel-1 change-detection flood map in GEE and assess its uncertainty.

How to go through the lesson

  1. Multispectral information – bands used for flood monitoring.
  2. Satellites characteristic – Landsat and Sentinel-2.
  3. Water indices – NDWI and MNDWI, with the GEE code for the optical part of the lab.
  4. Other water indexes – the wider family of indices.
  5. SAR backscatter analysis – why water is dark in radar images; loading Sentinel-1 in GEE.
  6. InSAR detection techniques – phase, DEMs, displacement.
  7. ICEYE – high temporal and spatial resolution SAR – commercial constellations and the two lab scripts (UN-SPIDER flood script).

This lesson is a short version of the course "Flood mapping approaches" by Jannik Jänichen and Kai Heckel on the Sentinel Hub education platform.
