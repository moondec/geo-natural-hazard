Milestone M5 – the landslide heatmap note from lab 5 (14.12), due 21.12, at the start of lab 6. A short report (.pdf or .docx, 2–4 pages) written to the template below, with one figure and the link to your saved script.

What to submit

  1. Choose an event with a known date and a reference inventory: one of the cases of Handwerger et al. (2022) (Hokkaido 2018, Hiroshima 2018, Haiti 2021) or an EMS landslide activation.
  2. Run the heatmap script with your AOI, event date, pre-event window (months), post-event window (weeks) and one Sentinel-1 orbit.
  3. Inspect the I_ratio layer on a known landslide and on an untouched slope and note the values.
  4. Vary the percentile threshold (99th, 90th, 80th), the kernel radius (1–3 km) and the length of the pre-event stack; describe how the heatmap changes.
  5. Overlay the reference (EMS grading map or a post-event Sentinel-2 true-colour image) and judge the agreement.
  6. Export the heatmap as GeoTIFF and write the note.

The step-by-step instructions are in the lesson landslides.

Report template – the chapters of your report

Use these headings in this order (3–6 pages; maps as numbered figures with captions). The same template is used for every homework of the course, so the points you learn here count every time.

  1. Title and metadata – event or area, dates, your name, the link to the saved script (test it in a private browser window).
  2. Aim – one sentence: what was mapped and why.
  3. Data – collections and their IDs, acquisition dates, the AOI (area in km², how it was defined), reference data.
    • Sentinel-1 orbit (ascending/descending), pre-event window and post-event window with the number of scenes in each
    • the reference inventory or EMS product (name, date)
  4. Method – the steps in the order of the script; every parameter and threshold with a one-sentence justification.
    • the log-ratio formula and its sign convention (positive = darker after the event)
    • masks: slope limit, water; the percentile threshold; kernel radius and cell size
  5. Results – the map(s) with legend, scale bar and source line; the numbers (tables in hectares, accuracies, counts).
    • one figure: the heatmap with the reference overlaid, AOI outline, scale bar
    • the I_ratio values you read on a landslide and on an untouched slope
  6. Validation and uncertainty – comparison with a reference and the sensitivity test where required.
    • which valleys agree with the reference and which do not
    • how the heatmap changed when you moved the percentile and the kernel radius
  7. Limitations and conclusions – clouds, phenology, resolution, speckle, sample quality – and two or three sentences on what the map can and cannot be used for.

Before you upload

The heatmap highlights the valleys that the reference shows as hit; I_ratio on untouched slopes stays near zero; flat valley floors are masked out; the GeoTIFF opens in QGIS with the right coordinates; the script link opens in a private browser window.
