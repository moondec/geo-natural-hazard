SAR Interferometry (InSAR) is a technique which was developed to infer 3D information from pairs of SAR images. Other than the technique of backscatter analysis, phase information of the respective SAR data is used. InSAR data exhibit very high sensitivity to ground features, allowing measurements of topographic height with an accuracy of a few meters and displacements with millimeter accuracies.


One method of InSAR contributing to flooding monitoring is the retrieval of accurate topographic information over large areas. Digital elevation data over most of the land areas were first obtained from data collected during the NASA Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM), which flew in 2000 aboard the Space Shuttle Endeavour. A second-generation global Digital Elevation Model (DEM) has been acquired by the TanDEM-X mission, flown by the German space agency (DLR).
