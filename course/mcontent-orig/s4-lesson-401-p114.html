Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) data are fundamental data sources for flood mapping.

Learn more about SAR

Due to their longer wavelengths, SAR images are commonly used for water retrieval as they are not affected by cloud cover, severe weather, and lighting conditions.

The following two images compare a normal situation with a flooding event. The first set of images show two different acquisitions of Sentinel-1 VH-Band from November 11, 2018 on the left and November 17, 2018 on the right. Extensive dark areas can be found out in the right picture, which can be identified as riverine flooded areas.


The second set of images show the same set in VV polarization. A new streamflow was detected and dark flooded areas are visible.


Since calm water strongly reflects radar wavelengths away from the receiving antenna (specular reflection), a backscatter analysis is a simple yet effective way for flood monitoring. Water-covered areas appear darker than land areas. Therefore, image thresholding is a straightforward way to identify water regions by their very low backscatter intensity.

Reasons for non-compliance include wind, which may roughen the water surface, or the presence of vegetation, as well as surrounding walls or other man-made structures, causing reflections. This kind of effect may cause backscatter values to increase.
